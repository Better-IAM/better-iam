Better IAM is an identity and access management platform delivered as TypeScript packages. It runs inside your
Node.js application, stores its records in your own PostgreSQL, SQLite, or libSQL database, and covers sign-in,
authorization, governance, and enterprise federation behind one typed API. This page compares that design with the
four kinds of tools teams usually reach for instead:

- **Hosted identity providers** such as Auth0, Clerk, and WorkOS.
- **Authentication libraries** such as Better Auth, Auth.js, and Lucia-style session code you write yourself.
- **Authorization services** such as OpenFGA, SpiceDB, Cerbos, Permit.io, and Oso.
- **Keycloak**, the open-source identity server.

The comparison sticks to architecture and to Better IAM facts you can check in this documentation. Other products
change quickly; check their current documentation for their feature lists.

## At a glance

| | Better IAM | Hosted identity providers | Authentication libraries | Authorization services | Keycloak |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | A library in your server process | A service the vendor operates | A library in your server process | A service you call (self-hosted or managed, depending on the product) | A standalone server you deploy |
| Where identity data lives | Your database | The vendor's infrastructure | Your database | Mostly authorization data (policies, relationship tuples, or synced attributes); sign-in lives elsewhere | A database Keycloak manages, separate from your application's |
| Main job | Sign-in, authorization, governance, federation | Sign-in, user management, enterprise SSO | Sign-in and sessions | Authorization decisions | Sign-in, SSO, identity brokering |
| How your code checks access | A function call: `iam.require(...)` reads current state from your database | Token claims or calls to the vendor's API, plus your own code | Your own code | A call to the decision service with the facts it needs | Token claims, or Keycloak's authorization services over HTTP |
| Language and runtime | TypeScript on Node.js 22.12 or newer | Any, through SDKs and standard protocols | The library's language | Any, through SDKs and APIs | Any, through OIDC and SAML |
| Who runs it | You | The vendor | You | You or the vendor | You |

## Compared with hosted identity providers

Auth0, Clerk, WorkOS, and similar services run identity for you. Your application redirects to, or embeds, the
vendor's sign-in experience, and the vendor stores the people, sessions, and organizations and keeps the service
running.

Better IAM moves that work into your application:

- **Data location.** People, sessions, roles, and audit records are rows in your database. Nothing leaves your
  infrastructure unless you configure an integration that sends it (webhooks, SCIM outbound, Shared Signals).
- **Authorization next to your data.** A check such as "may Alice approve this invoice?" runs in your process and
  can use the invoice's owner and amount through `resolveResource`, instead of copying that data into tokens or a
  vendor's user metadata.
- **No hosted pages.** You build the sign-in screens with the typed client, the React, Vue, or Svelte bindings, or
  the Next.js form helpers. The repository includes a reference administration console (`apps/console`) built on the
  public API, but it is an example application, not a managed dashboard.
- **You operate it.** Upgrades, database backups, the secret, scheduled jobs (email outbox, retention sweep), and
  availability are your responsibility. See [Operations](/docs/operations).

## Compared with authentication libraries

Better Auth, Auth.js, and hand-written session code also run in your process against your database, so the
deployment model is the same. The difference is scope: an authentication library answers "who is this?", and
Better IAM also answers "what may they do, who granted it, and who reviewed it?".

- **Tenancy model.** In Better IAM each organization is a tenant with its own identity directory, its own roles, its
  own sign-in policy, and its own SSO connections. In Better Auth's organization plugin, one user record joins many
  organizations as a member. Neither is wrong; Better IAM's model fits B2B products where each customer administers
  its own people.
- **Authorization engine.** Decisions come from roles, JSON policies with conditions, relationships, and boundaries,
  evaluated by one engine on the server and testable in the [policy playground](/playground). An authentication
  library leaves that to your application code or to an add-on.
- **Governance and audit.** Just-in-time elevation, access reviews, separation of duties, and a tamper-evident audit
  chain are built in, and every operation, including an administrator's, runs through the same authorization and
  audit pipeline.
- **Cost of the extra scope.** Better IAM has more concepts to learn (tenants, catalogs, bindings, authorities) and
  more tables in your database. If you only need individual users to sign in, an authentication library is less to
  run.

Better IAM is independent of Better Auth; it does not use or extend it. For moving users across, see the
[migration guides](/docs/guides/migration).

## Compared with authorization services

OpenFGA and SpiceDB (relationship graphs in the style of Google's Zanzibar), Cerbos (a policy decision point), and
Permit.io and Oso (authorization platforms) decide what a caller may do. They do not sign people in: your application
authenticates the caller elsewhere, then sends the decision service the facts it needs, such as relationship tuples
you keep in sync or principal and resource attributes on each request.

Better IAM combines both halves in one process:

- **One source of truth.** The identities, groups, roles, and relationships the decision uses are the same records
  that sign-in, SCIM provisioning, and access reviews change, so nothing has to be synchronized into a second
  system.
- **Decisions on current state.** Every check reads your database, not a cache, so a revoked role or session stops
  working on the very next request. Batch with `authorizeMany` (up to 50 checks per call) and list with
  `listAccessible` to keep pages fast. See [How decisions work](/docs/authorization/how-decisions-work).
- **Scope.** A dedicated authorization service is built to be the single decision point for many services written in
  many languages. Better IAM's engine runs in a Node.js process; other services rely on the decisions, signed
  assertions, or tokens it issues.

## Compared with Keycloak

Keycloak is a mature open-source identity server. You deploy it next to your applications, configure realms,
clients, and identity providers in its admin console or REST API, and your applications sign people in through
OIDC or SAML. Better IAM covers similar ground (tenants comparable to realms, SSO, SCIM, an OAuth/OIDC provider, an
LDAP gateway) with a different shape:

- **Embedded, not standalone.** Better IAM is a dependency of your Node.js application, configured in TypeScript,
  migrated with `better-iam migrate`, and called with typed functions. There is no separate server to deploy, secure,
  or theme.
- **Application authorization.** Policies, relationships, and resource attributes from your own records are first
  class, so the same engine that signs people in also enforces `documents:write` on one document in your routes.
- **Language reach.** Keycloak serves applications in any language through standard protocols. Better IAM's server
  APIs are TypeScript; applications in other languages integrate through its OAuth/OIDC provider, SAML, SCIM, or the
  signed session JWTs it publishes at `/api/iam/.well-known/jwks.json` when session JWTs are enabled.

## When to choose something else

Better IAM is a good fit when access control, multi-tenancy, and auditability are part of your product and your
backend runs on Node.js. Choose something else when:

- **You do not want to operate identity.** A hosted identity provider runs the service, the database, and the
  sign-in pages for you, and the vendor carries the operational and compliance work for that service.
- **You need a ready-made sign-in UI and user dashboard today.** Hosted providers ship them. With Better IAM you
  build your screens on the client and framework helpers.
- **Your application only signs individuals in.** With no organizations, custom roles, or enterprise customers, an
  authentication library such as Better Auth or Auth.js is smaller and simpler.
- **Your backend is not Node.js, or many services in different languages need one decision point.** Keycloak, a hosted
  provider, or an authorization service such as OpenFGA, SpiceDB, or Cerbos fits a polyglot backend better.
- **Authorization is your only problem and the relationship graph is very large.** A Zanzibar-style service is built
  for that single job at that scale.
- **You need a long-stable API.** Better IAM is at version 0.3, before 1.0, so minor releases can change APIs. The
  [upgrade guides](/docs/guides/upgrade) list what changed and how to move.

If you are moving from one of these tools, note that password hashes are not imported: Better IAM hashes passwords
with argon2id and `identities.createMany` accepts only plain-text passwords. People who move over set a password
through the reset flow, or sign in with a magic link, a passkey, or your enterprise identity provider. The
[migration recipe](/docs/guides/recipes/tenancy-and-limits#move-people-over-from-another-system) walks through a
complete move.
