License management: products, pools of seats, assignments to people and groups, and seats with a waiting list.
A product (a SKU, such as a plan tier or an add-on) is defined by the platform or by a tenant, pools give each tenant a
number of seats of it, and the product is assigned to people and groups. Better IAM keeps one seat per person, active
while capacity lasts and otherwise waiting in order, and moves seats the moment anything changes. What a license
unlocks is expressed with existing governance: policies on `principal.licenses`, birthright package rules on
`identity.licenses`, and the product's feature keys. The repository guide is `docs/licenses.md`.

## Products and where they apply

A product defined on the root tenant is a platform product: every tenant sees and assigns it, and only root
administrators define, change, or retire it. A product defined by an organization or project is visible to that tenant
and every tenant below it. Keys are permanent, unique in the defining tenant, and a tenant cannot repeat a key an
enclosing tenant defines; when an enclosing tenant later introduces a key a tenant below already uses, the enclosing
product wins and the lower one is listed as `shadowed`: its seats stay but no longer grant the key or its feature keys.
Only the defining tenant changes a product. Retiring a product releases its seats in every tenant and leaves its pools
and assignments as read-only history.

Capacity is granted by whoever owns the product: root administrators grant seats of platform products, and a tenant's
managers grant seats of its own products to itself or to any tenant below it. Pool changes are therefore authorized in
the product's defining tenant, while their `license:pool-*` events are recorded in the receiving tenant.

## Seats and the waiting list

The claimants of a product in a tenant are its direct assignees and the live members of assigned groups; only active,
unexpired identities of the tenant claim a seat, of any kind (people, service accounts, agents, guests). They are
ranked by seniority, the first time each person claimed the product: the first `capacity` hold an active seat (the sum
of the tenant's live pools) and the rest wait in order. Within one millisecond, existing active seats rank before
existing waiting seats, which rank before new claimants, so a newcomer never takes a seat someone holds. Capacity is
never over-allocated: a cut moves the newest active seats to the head of the waiting list instead of failing.

Seats move in the same transaction as assignments, pool changes, retirement, group membership changes from any
writer, group deletion, and identity deletion; disabling and offboarding release seats right after the change. Pool
terms and expiring memberships or accounts are applied by the hourly job
[`iam.licenses.reconcile`](/docs/reference/api#license-jobs-and-helpers). Each change is audited on the person as
`license:seat-activate`, `license:seat-waiting`, or `license:seat-release`, with `from`, `to`, and a `reason`, and
methods that move seats return them as `seats` (`tenantId`, `productId`, `productKey`, `identityId`, `from`, `to`).

## What a license unlocks

- **Policies.** `principal.licenses` lists the keys of the products the principal holds an active seat for in the
  decision's tenant (see [principal keys](/docs/guides/authorization/conditions#principal-keys)). Waiting seats,
  retired products, and seats held only through a lapsed group membership never count; assumed roles hold none.
  Simulations, who-can reviews, and access invariants see the real seats, and `policies.test` defaults the key to an
  empty list.
- **Birthright packages.** Package rules test `identity.licenses` with product keys (see
  [automatic assignment](/docs/guides/privileged-access/automatic-assignment)), so a seat brings the roles and groups
  a product needs and takes them away when it goes. Seats held only through package-granted memberships do not count.
- **Feature keys.** `iam.licenses.features` returns the feature keys a person's active seats unlock for server code,
  counting only platform products (defined by the root tenant) unless you pass `trustedTenantId`, so no tenant can
  mint a paid feature for itself by defining a product that lists its key. [`mine`](#mine) returns them to the person,
  with the platform's keys in `featureKeys` and every product's in `allFeatureKeys`.

Assigning, unassigning, and pool changes run the receiving tenant's enforced
[access invariants](/docs/guides/governance/change-safety#access-invariants), so a seat change that newly breaks one
fails with `INVARIANT_VIOLATION`.

## Who may call what

Reading needs `iam:licenses:read`, handing out seats `iam:licenses:assign`, and defining products, adding capacity,
and settings `iam:licenses:manage`. They are checked on `iam/licenses/products` (with `/{id}` for one product),
`iam/licenses/pools` (with `/{id}` for one pool), `iam/licenses/assignments`, `iam/licenses/seats`, and
`iam/licenses/settings`. Every call is audited under its permission (reads included), and changes also record the
`license:*` event each method names. [`mine`](#mine) needs no permission and is not audited.

A product assigned to a group goes to whoever joins it, so every other API that puts people into a licensed group, or
keeps them there longer, asks for `iam:licenses:assign` on `iam/licenses/assignments` too: the groups API,
`identities.createMany` and `invite` (checked again at acceptance and when resent), guest invitations and
`guests.convertToMember` with `clearExpiry`, access packages and their rules, onboarding completion groups, the teams
API for administrators, and configuration sync. A team's maintainers, directory sync (SCIM), and renewing a guest
(`guests.attest`) are exempt by design.

## updatePool

Changes a pool's quantity, its end, or its note; seats follow the new capacity at once.

<ApiEndpoint group="licenses" method="updatePool" http="POST /licenses/updatePool" credential="required" />

- **Permission:** `iam:licenses:manage` on `iam/licenses/pools/{id}` in the product's defining tenant; for a platform
  product, a root administrator.
- **Audited as:** `iam:licenses:manage`, `license:pool-update` in the receiving tenant (metadata: `before`, `after`),
  and the seat changes.
- **Errors:** `INVALID_INPUT` with nothing to change, for a `quantity` outside 1 to 1,000,000, or an `endsAt` not
  after `startsAt`; `NOT_FOUND` when the pool is not the tenant's; `ACCESS_DENIED` for anyone but the defining tenant's
  managers; `INVALID_TRANSITION` (409) for a retired product; `INVARIANT_VIOLATION` as for `addPool`.

`endsAt: null` makes the pool open-ended and a past time ends it now; `note: null` clears the note. A capacity cut
never fails: the newest active seats move to the head of the waiting list. The result carries the pool and the
`seats` that moved.

```ts title="Signature"
iam.api.licenses.updatePool(
  credential: CredentialInput,
  input: {
    tenantId: string;
    poolId: string;
    quantity?: number;
    endsAt?: number | null;
    note?: string | null;
  },
): Promise<LicensePoolResult>
```

## updateProduct

Renames a product, changes its description, or replaces its feature keys; the key never changes.

<ApiEndpoint group="licenses" method="updateProduct" http="POST /licenses/updateProduct" credential="required" />

- **Permission:** `iam:licenses:manage` on `iam/licenses/products/{id}` in the defining tenant; for a platform
  product, a root administrator.
- **Audited as:** `iam:licenses:manage` and `license:product-update` (metadata: `before`, `after`).
- **Errors:** `INVALID_INPUT` with nothing to change, for an invalid name, description, or feature key, or when an
  enclosing tenant defines the product (change it there); `NOT_FOUND` when it is not visible; `ACCESS_DENIED` for a
  platform product without root.

`description: null` clears the description, and `featureKeys` replaces the whole list. New feature keys apply to every
active seat at once: in [`mine`](#mine) (`featureKeys` for a platform product, `allFeatureKeys` always) and in
`iam.licenses.features` for a platform product, or for a tenant's product where the caller trusts that tenant
(`trustedTenantId`). No recent sign-in is needed.

```ts title="Signature"
iam.api.licenses.updateProduct(
  credential: CredentialInput,
  input: {
    tenantId: string;
    productId: string;
    name?: string;

    featureKeys?: string[];
  },
): Promise<LicenseProductView>
```

## usage

Reports, per product the tenant uses, its capacity and how many seats are active, waiting, available, and reclaimable.

<ApiEndpoint group="licenses" method="usage" http="POST /licenses/usage" credential="required" />

- **Permission:** `iam:licenses:read` on `iam/licenses/seats`.
- **Audited as:** `iam:licenses:read`.

A product is listed when the tenant has pools, seats, or assignments of it, or defines it. Each entry has `capacity`
(from live pools), `active`, `waiting`, `available`, live `pools`, `assignments`, and with reclaim on,
`reclaimable` (active seats older than `reclaimAfterDays` whose holders have been inactive that long) and
`reclaimableThroughGroups` (the part held only through groups, which the reclaim job leaves: remove those people from
the group). The result is `{ tenantId, reclaimAfterDays, products }`.

<ApiShape id="licenses.usage" http />

```ts title="Signature"
iam.api.licenses.usage(
  credential: CredentialInput,
  input: { tenantId: string },
): Promise<LicenseUsageReport>
```
